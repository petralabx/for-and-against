# Brand voice & positioning

**Last verified:** 2026-07-14 · Links: [scents](scents/README.md) · [competitors](../competitors/README.md) · [claims](../compliance/claims.md)

## Positioning

Premium body care, positioned as an accessible-luxury alternative to Salt & Stone. For & Against exists to deliver the same luxury feel, performance, packaging, and fragrances as elevated brands (Salt & Stone, Ouai, Necessaire) without the unnecessary markup. Integrated manufacturing — we own the facility and fill every bottle — is what makes the price point possible, and it's a core proof point in copy.

The founding argument, in the brand's own words: the best-performing luxury body washes cost $36 a bottle. "That's not a formula problem. That's a business model problem."

## Audience

Health-conscious people aged 25–45, likely urban, who care about wellness, performance, fashion, and bathroom/brand aesthetic. They know what premium feels like and resent paying for marketing rather than product.

## Voice

Elevated, minimal, confident. Not clinical, not cutesy, not over-explained. Simple, elegant language that connects with the everyday person. No fluff.

**Recurring constructions that are on-voice** (drawn from live copy):
- Short declarative openers: "Smells expensive. Performs like it too."
- "Stripped back to what actually matters."
- "No overcomplicated claims. No inflated price. Just a luxury [product] that works."
- "Without the premium price tag." / "Skip the markup."
- "Refill. Reuse. Repeat."
- Sign-off: "For & Against. Exactly what it says."

**Off-voice:** exclamation-heavy enthusiasm, clinical jargon dumps, wellness woo, apologetic hedging, anything that over-explains.
